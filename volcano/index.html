Part 1

How volcanoes form

Volcanoes are not random mountains. They are places where melted rock, trapped gases, and moving tectonic plates work together.

Tectonic Settings Explorer

Choose a setting to see where the magma comes from and which kind of volcano is most likely to form.

Convergent boundaries often make explosive volcanoes.

When a dense oceanic plate sinks beneath another plate, water and heat help mantle rock melt. The magma is usually sticky and gas-rich, so pressure builds before eruption.

Key scientific ideas

Subduction One tectonic plate sinks beneath another, carrying water into the mantle.
Melting Water lowers the melting point of nearby rock, producing magma.
Volcano type Steep composite volcanoes often form where eruptions alternate ash and lava.
Remember this rule

Plate movement creates pathways for magma. Volcanoes need a source of melted rock and a route to the surface.

Why eruptions change

The chemistry of magma matters. Silica changes viscosity, and trapped gas changes pressure.

Eruption style: Explosive stratovolcano pattern

High silica makes magma thicker. When gas cannot escape easily, pressure builds until the eruption blasts out ash, lava fragments, and gases.

Viscosity: High Viscosity is a liquid's resistance to flowing. Thick magma moves slowly.
Gas pressure: High Gas bubbles expand as magma rises. If the magma is sticky, the gas stays trapped longer.
Part 3

Volcanic activity and monitoring

Scientists cannot stop eruptions, but they can watch for warning signs: earthquakes, swelling ground, hotter rocks, and changing gas output.

Volcano Watch Dashboard

Alert level: Watch

Some signs are increasing, so scientists would collect more data. A warning sign means magma may be moving underground, not that an eruption is guaranteed today.

Volcano states

Active

Has erupted recently

An active volcano is erupting now or has erupted in the recent past and is still likely to erupt again.

Dormant

Sleeping, not dead

A dormant volcano has not erupted for a long time but can erupt again if fresh magma rises.

Extinct

Very unlikely to erupt

An extinct volcano no longer has a reliable magma supply, so an eruption is considered extremely unlikely.

What scientists measure Seismometers track earthquakes, GPS tracks swelling, and gas sensors detect sulfur dioxide and carbon dioxide.
Important idea One signal alone is not enough. Scientists compare multiple clues before raising alerts.
Part 4

Vocabulary practice

Use these flashcards before the quiz. Click each button to reveal the definition.

Magma

Molten rock beneath Earth's surface.

Lava

Magma after it erupts onto the surface.

Vent

An opening through which lava, ash, and gas escape.

Crater

A bowl-shaped opening at the top of many volcanoes.

Subduction

One tectonic plate sinks beneath another.

Hot spot

A place where mantle heat rises through the crust.

Viscosity

How much a fluid resists flowing.

Tephra

Rock fragments blasted into the air by an eruption.
