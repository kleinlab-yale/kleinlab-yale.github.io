What the model encodes

The ALK cartoon is reduced to the features you described as decisive: the membrane, the EGF-like spacer, and a three-sphere GRD made only of handle, pole, and PXL. It also follows the Yale “protein selfie-stick” framing in which the handle binds ligand, the pole organizes the ectodomain, and the PXL side helps capture the second receptor.

Structural anchors

The viewer is grounded in the ligand-reception paper The structural basis for ALK1 and LTK receptor recognition and activation by ALKALs and the membrane-orientation / activation paper Mechanism for the activation of the anaplastic lymphoma kinase receptor. Representative PDBs: 7LIP, 7LI8, 7LIT, 7LID, 7LIQ, 7ME5, 7ME6, 7ME2, and 7ME7.

Mechanistic logic

In the ALKAL-bound view, the EGF-like domain acts as a spacer that lifts the handle off the membrane and creates a narrow ligand pocket. The handle, pole, and PXL then lie as a simple membrane-parallel GRD, while the ligand projects orthogonally to that three-sphere axis so it can help stitch the neighboring GRD. The activation paper further argues that this EGF-like repositioning is required because the linker to the transmembrane helix is too short to support productive dimerization without that movement.

Scope

This is a mechanistic viewer, not an atom-by-atom reconstruction. It is intended to make the membrane-parallel GRD pose and the nearly symmetric dimerization logic immediately legible in the same visual style as the ROS1 site, while using a single generic ALKAL depiction for the family of activating ligands.
