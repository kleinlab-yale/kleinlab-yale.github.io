What the model encodes

The ALK cartoon is reduced to the features you described as decisive: the membrane, the EGF-like spacer, the GRD ligand-binding surface, the small ALKAL ligand, and the symmetric dimer contact that turns signaling on. It also follows the Yale “protein selfie-stick” framing of a ligand-binding handle, a rigid pole, and a PXL sensor that helps capture the second receptor.

Structural anchors

The viewer is grounded in the ligand-reception paper The structural basis for ALK1 and LTK receptor recognition and activation by ALKALs and the membrane-orientation / activation paper Mechanism for the activation of the anaplastic lymphoma kinase receptor.

Mechanistic logic

In the ALKAL-bound view, the EGF-like domain acts as a spacer that lifts the GRD off the membrane and creates a narrow ligand pocket. The GRD then lies broadly parallel to the membrane surface, while a small exposed part of the bound ligand remains available to help contact the neighboring ALK complex. The activation paper further argues that this EGF-like repositioning is required because the linker to the transmembrane helix is too short to support productive dimerization without that movement.

Scope

This is a mechanistic viewer, not an atom-by-atom reconstruction. It is intended to make the membrane-parallel GRD pose and the nearly symmetric dimerization logic immediately legible in the same visual style as the ROS1 site, while using a single generic ALKAL depiction for the family of activating ligands.
