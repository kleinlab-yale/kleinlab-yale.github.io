Interactive RTK Teaching App

Weak dimerization can stretch signaling into a long, low plateau until receptor abundance tips the same system into a burst.

This GitHub Pages-ready app is a qualitative teaching model for how receptor abundance, dimerization strength, cooperativity, and internalization reshape RTK output. It is tuned to illustrate the narrative you described from your Molecular Cell paper: low sustained signaling can bias differentiation, while high transient signaling can bias growth.

Model note: this interface is deliberately simplified for students; it captures the qualitative logic rather than a full methods-level reconstruction.

Cooperativity regime

Negative cooperativity is represented as a broader, weaker dimerization response; positive cooperativity is sharper and stronger.

Trafficking

Strong activation drives receptor loss from the surface, which shortens the pulse.

Model structure

The simulator treats ligand occupancy, receptor dimerization, activation, internalization, and recycling as a compact dynamical system. It uses three ideas:

  1. Surface receptors bind a transient ligand pulse: bound = R_surface * L / (L + K_L)
  2. Active dimers emerge from a cooperativity-shaped dimerization curve controlled by receptor abundance and Kd: dimerized ~ bound * f(bound / Kd, cooperativity)
  3. Active dimers drive both signal and receptor internalization: dR_surface/dt = -k_int * active + k_rec * internal
