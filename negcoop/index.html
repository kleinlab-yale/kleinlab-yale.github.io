Model structure

This app uses a reduced phenomenological RTK model rather than a full mechanistic reaction network. Time is simulated from 0 to 60 minutes in 0.25 minute steps, with a fixed 1 minute ligand pulse starting at time zero.

  1. Ligand and effective dimerization drive. The pulse is represented as L(t) = L0 / (1 + exp((t - 1) / 0.12)), receptor occupancy as theta(t) = L(t) / (L(t) + 0.7), and peak dimerization drive as D = R * theta_peak / (Kd * c_coop). The cooperativity setting changes c_coop and adds regime-specific bias toward transient or sustained output.
  2. Two-component signaling response. The signal is written as the sum of a fast transient branch and a weak sustained branch. The transient term uses a gamma-like peak, g(t) = (t / t_peak) * exp(1 - t / t_peak), multiplied by an early shutoff gate. The sustained term is the product of a rise logistic and a fall logistic. Their amplitudes are heuristic functions of D, receptor abundance, and a near-threshold weak-Kd window, which allows weak dimerization to remain low and prolonged at low receptor abundance but switch to a high transient when receptor abundance is increased.
  3. Trafficking and signal collapse. Internalized receptor fraction follows I(t + dt) = clamp(I + [kint * (T + 0.22S) * (1 - I) - krec * I]dt) . Surface receptors are Rs = R * (1 - I), and displayed signaling is scaled by surface availability, signal = (T + S) * (0.58 + 0.42 * Rs / R). This produces rapid decay after a strong burst when internalization outpaces recycling.
  4. Summary metrics and labels. Peak signal, peak time, area-under-the-curve-derived effective duration, and final surface receptor loss are calculated from the simulated trace. The transient versus sustained label comes from a score that increases with peak amplitude and receptor loss and decreases with effective duration, so it should be interpreted as a compact teaching summary of the waveform rather than a direct fate prediction.

Limitations

  • Parameters are in relative units and are tuned for qualitative behavior, not globally fit to a single experimental dataset.
  • The simulator does not explicitly solve monomer-dimer mass-action equations with ligand depletion, adaptor competition, phosphatases, endosomal signaling, or downstream ERK/AKT feedback loops.
  • Cooperativity is represented by effective Kd rescaling and bias terms rather than a microscopic thermodynamic model of receptor-receptor interaction energies.
  • The growth-like and differentiation-like labels summarize curve shape in this reduced model and should not be treated as universal predictions across all ligands, cell types, or RTKs.
